History

From RAG Hype to RAG Accountability

RAG was sold as the fix for hallucination. It took four years — and a word-level annotated corpus — for anyone to measure how much hallucination actually survives inside a RAG pipeline.

2020 · May
RAG introduced (Lewis et al.)
Retrieval-augmented generation bolts retrieved passages onto the prompt, promising to fix hallucinations caused by stale parametric knowledge.
2022–2023
RAG goes mainstream
Bing Chat and Google Bard ship retrieval-grounded answers; enterprises wire LLMs to their document stores. "Grounded" quietly becomes a synonym for "safe".
2022–2023
Hallucination benchmarks — but not for RAG
Existing hallucination datasets label whole sentences or answers, and are not grounded in retrieved evidence — useless for asking "which exact words contradict the context?"
2023
Detection without ground truth
SelfCheckGPT samples extra answers and checks consistency; LMvLM cross-examines with judge models. There is no shared RAG testbed to score any of them on.
2024 · Jan
🚀 RAGTruth (Niu et al.)
17,790 naturally generated RAG responses from 6 LLMs, 14,289 hallucinated spans annotated at word level, and a conflict-vs-baseless taxonomy for every span.
2024 →
A measuring stick for RAG reliability
Published at ACL 2024, RAGTruth becomes shared training and evaluation data for hallucination detectors — and proof that a fine-tuned 13B model out-detects GPT-4 prompting.
Key Insight

Retrieval reduces hallucination — it does not remove it. In RAGTruth, 43.1% of responses contain at least one hallucinated span, even though every prompt already contained the retrieved evidence the model was supposed to stick to.

Chapter 01

The Problem — Benchmarks That Can't See RAG

Before RAGTruth, you could say "this answer is hallucinated" — but not which words were wrong, not whether they contradicted the evidence or simply invented details, and not how often RAG systems hallucinate in the wild.

🚫
Hallucination Benchmarks Before RAGTruth
  • Sentence- or whole-answer labels — no way to localize which words are wrong
  • Not grounded in retrieved context — no evidence to check claims against
  • Often constructed or templated, not naturally generated RAG outputs
  • Detection methods had no shared, RAG-specific testbed to compare on
  • Prompt-based GPT-4 detection reached only 18.4% precision at span level
🔎
RAGTruth's Answer
  • Word-level span annotations — the exact hallucinated phrases are marked
  • Every response ships with the retrieved context it was generated from
  • Naturally generated by 6 real LLMs, from GPT-4 to Llama-2 and Mistral
  • Each span typed: conflict vs. baseless, split into evident and subtle
  • Two annotators per response — 91.8% response-level, 78.8% span-level consistency

Chapter 02

Building the Corpus

No templates, no synthetic errors. RAGTruth collects what real models actually say when given real retrieved context — then has humans mark every hallucinated span, word by word.

STEP 1
Pick tasks & sources
MS MARCO questions, Yelp business JSON, CNN/DM articles and recent news — each item keeps its retrieved context.
STEP 2
Prompt 6 LLMs
GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Llama-2-7B/13B/70B-chat and Mistral-7B-Instruct each answer every input — 6 responses per sample, grounded in the same context.
STEP 3
Two annotators per response
Each marks the exact hallucinated spans at word level and types them into the four-type taxonomy. Consistency: 91.8% response-level, 78.8% span-level.
STEP 4
Third review
Wherever the two annotations differ considerably, a third reviewer arbitrates — the paper's safeguard against noisy labels.

A Strict Definition of Hallucination

RAGTruth takes the strict RAG view: anything the retrieved context does not support is a hallucination — even if it happens to be true in the real world. A response mentioning a detail that is plausible, commonly known, but absent from the evidence is still flagged.

implicit_true
13.5% of spans are information not in the reference but possibly true — still labeled, so users can keep or drop them per their own tolerance.
due_to_null
17.7% of data-to-text stems treat JSON "null" as "yes" — models invent attributes the data simply doesn't know.

These extra labels make the corpus adaptive: strict for safety-critical RAG, lenient for casual chat.

Chapter 03

Two Kinds of Wrong — Conflict vs. Baseless

Every hallucinated span is sorted into one of two major categories, each with an evident and a subtle flavor. This is what makes RAGTruth diagnostic, not just a score.

CATEGORY 1 — CONFLICT

The response contradicts the retrieved context. The evidence says X; the model says not-X.

⚠ EVIDENT CONFLICT — direct opposition
Original: "…sent about 500 of the most seriously injured people for treatment in Kathmandu already — the majority by road."
Generated: "The team airlifted 500 injured people…"
🌫 SUBTLE CONFLICT — meaning shift
Original: marks that are "immoral, scandalous, or disparaging"
Generated: prohibits marks that are "immoral, scandalous, or disqualified."
CATEGORY 2 — BASELESS

The response adds details the context never said. Not a contradiction — an invention.

⚠ EVIDENT BASELESS — fabricated detail
Original: "She had no contact with the surviving members of the band until the 2006 premiere…"
Generated: "The couple divorced in 1968, but later reconciled and attended the 2006…"
🌫 SUBTLE BASELESS — invented specifics
Original: "Amazing happy hour and great people."
Generated: "happy hour, which is available daily from 4 pm to 7 pm."

Why Data-to-Text Is the Worst (68.6%)

Writing a business overview from sparse JSON is brutal: the evidence is mostly true, false and null fields. Models fill the empty space with plausible-sounding details — ambience, music, crowds — that the data never contained. About 17.7% of data-to-text hallucination stems directly from treating null as "yes".

Longer Answers, More Hallucination

In question answering, the average number of hallucinations per response climbs from 0.21 in the shortest response bucket to 0.87 in the longest — roughly 4× more. Every extra sentence is another chance to drift off the evidence. RAG answers should stay short.

Bonus: the Detector as a Gatekeeper

Generate two candidate responses, keep the one the detector flags less. For the open-source group (Llama-2-7B + Mistral-7B), the hallucination rate fell from 52.4% to 41.1% picking the cleaner response — and to 19.3% when only accepting responses with zero detected spans (−63.2%). Even the GPT-3.5/GPT-4 group dropped from 9.8% to 4.8%.

What RAGTruth Did NOT Solve
  • Span-level detection is still weak overall — even the fine-tuned model reaches only 52.7 F1, and subtle hallucinations are harder to catch than evident ones
  • The strict setting flags possibly-true information as hallucination (13.5% of spans) — deliberate, but not universally the right call
  • English-only, three task families; no open-domain dialogue or code
  • The corpus measures word-level hallucination, not attribution quality or ranking of retrieved passages
Legacy

Impact — Grounded Accountability

RAGTruth turned "my RAG system hallucinates sometimes" into a measurable, trainable, comparable claim. Research on RAG reliability has had a shared ruler ever since.

📏 A ruler for RAG reliability
The first large corpus of naturally generated RAG responses with word-level hallucination labels — grounded in the retrieved context each response was actually generated from.
🎯 Span-level supervision
Word-level labels let detectors be trained and scored on localization, not just flagging — the difference between "this answer is bad" and "these words are wrong".
🏋️ Detector training data
A fine-tuned Llama-2-13B hit 78.7 response-level F1 — beating GPT-4-turbo prompting (63.4) at a fraction of the inference cost. Small models, well supervised, win.
🔁 A reusable testbed
The paper's shared 450-instance test set scored prompt-based, SelfCheckGPT, LMvLM and fine-tuned baselines side by side — the template later detector papers follow.
🗂️ A taxonomy worth keeping
Conflict vs. baseless (each evident or subtle) gives RAG teams a vocabulary for triage: contradictions need re-retrieval, inventions need tighter prompting or truncation.
⚠️ The sobering lesson
43.1% of responses hallucinated with gold context attached, and even GPT-4 sat at 9.3%. "It has RAG" is never a sufficient answer to "can we trust it?".
Deep Dive

The Strict Contract: If the Context Doesn't Say It, It's a Hallucination

RAGTruth's definitional move sounds harsh and is deliberate: in a RAG system, an answer is only allowed to come from the retrieved evidence. Unsupported-but-true is still labeled hallucination — because the user cannot trace it, the system cannot defend it, and the habit of untraceable claims is exactly what trustworthy generation must break.

📏
What Word-Level Spans Buy
  • 17,790 naturally generated RAG responses from 6 LLMs — failures found in the wild, not hand-built
  • 14,289 annotated spans at word level: 43.1% of responses contain hallucination
  • Four span types: evident/subtle conflict (contradicts evidence) and evident/subtle baseless (adds unsupported claims)
  • Three task families: QA (MS MARCO), data-to-text (Yelp), summarization (CNN/DM + news)
🚨
The Numbers Nobody Wanted
  • 43.1% of RAG responses hallucinate — retrieval reduces, not eliminates
  • Open-source model group peaked at 52.4% hallucination rate — worse than coin-flip reliability
  • Zero-shot GPT-4-turbo detection loses to a fine-tuned Llama-2-13B (78.7 vs lower response-level F1)
  • Even with detector-guided selection, 19.3% of responses still hallucinate — RAG needs verification as a stage, not a hope

VERDICT
A fine-tuned 13B beats GPT-4-turbo at zero-shot — because the task is not reasoning, it's alignment checking
Span detection rewards exactly one skill: does this phrase follow from these words? That's a learned discrimination, not a reasoning feat — which is why the Llama-2-13B fine-tune reached 78.7 response-level F1 where prompting a frontier model fell short. The endgame the paper proved: use the detector as a selection gate (52.4% → 19.3% hallucination in the open-source group). Where this continues: SelfCheckGPT gates by sampling, FActScore gates atomically — the three papers are one strategy with different gates.
🧾 Strict RAG setting
Even plausible-and-true additions are labeled hallucination without evidence support. Harsh by design: traceability is the product a trustworthy system sells.
🎨 Four span types
Evident conflict (flat contradiction), subtle conflict (twisted detail), evident baseless (invented from nothing), subtle baseless (plausible-but-ungrounded). Subtle spans are where detectors bleed F1.
🏭 Naturally generated
Spans were found in real model outputs across 6 LLMs — not adversarially authored. 43.1% is the ambient rate, not a worst case.
🔌 The gate, not the cure
Detector-guided selection cuts hallucination 52.4% → 19.3% and regenerating when the gate trips is the shipped pattern — verification as a pipeline stage. The upstream half of the story is the RAG paper in Track II.

Key Takeaways

Everything you need to remember about this paper.

✅ 17,790 naturally generated RAG responses from 6 LLMs, with 14,289 hallucinated spans annotated at word level — 43.1% of responses hallucinate.
✅ Three task families: question answering (MS MARCO), data-to-text writing (Yelp) and news summarization (CNN/DM + recent news).
